# PDP-6 AAP logical ticks for optimization (2026-10-15, v1)

## Decision

Historical nanosecond/microsecond calibration is **not a prerequisite** to
optimization. Within an unchanged AAP model, configuration and workload,
logical pulse-delay ticks are a relative machine-cost proxy. The simulator
is not verified real PDP-6 hardware; timings must not be labeled seconds.
SIMH's exact executed-opcode histogram remains an independent comparison
metric and correctness remains the prerequisite for accepting improvements.

## Measurement boundary

The initial diagnostic trace provides `IT0` instruction-cycle entry and
`ET0A` execution-phase milestones. Neither `ET0A` nor the interval
`IT0` to `ET0A` proves complete instruction duration. For consecutively
paired, monotonic straight-line traces, measure

`instruction_cost_i = IT0_tick_(i+1) - IT0_tick_i`.

The interval includes the intervening execution and the transition to
the next instruction, but its attribution remains provisional on unusual
flows. The final instruction is explicitly **unmeasured** without a later
IT0 boundary; never fill it from an estimated `ET0A` difference. The
strict `tools/aap-tick-cost-v1.py` produces versioned JSON and rejects
unsupported or malformed milestone ordering rather than guessing.

For comparable profiles, supply `--workload-id` and `--model-id` to the
extractor. The latter should identify the AAP git revision and memory
configuration. `tools/compare-aap-tick-cost-v1.py BASELINE.json
CANDIDATE.json` compares the sum of complete intervals and fails on a cost
increase by default; `--max-regression-pct N` provides an explicit tolerance.
The comparator refuses mismatched identifiers, incomplete accounting, or
inconsistent data. Identifiers do not themselves establish correctness or
workload equivalence: these must be tested independently first.

The validated MOVEI/ADDI/JRST halt guest provides two complete intervals:
MOVEI 1,401 logical ticks and ADDI 1,951 logical ticks in the observed
run. The terminating JRST has no next IT0 and is excluded from the total.
These are example-model values, **not historical microseconds or universal
per-opcode constants**.

## Integration and acceptance gates

1. Maintain repeatable, passing guest and correctness tests in the testkit.
2. Validate branches, taken/not-taken conditions, indirect/indexed memory,
   `XCT`, interrupts and more complex opcode families before attempting
   general per-opcode cost tables. Measure under controlled configurations.
3. Require common emulator revision/configuration and identical test inputs
   for baseline/candidate comparisons. Report raw instruction counts,
   modeled tick costs, and unmeasured coverage separately.
4. Do not use tick sums as a performance gate when incomplete instruction
   boundaries or mismatched workloads would make comparisons misleading.
5. Keep physical-time calibration as optional future research. Any cost
   model derived from traces must record its origin and uncertainty.

**Current state:** strict interval extraction, executing guest regression,
and model-matched tick-cost comparison are integrated. Branch, XCT and
indirect-flow validation, comprehensive opcode calibration, and matrix-wide
weighted optimization are not yet completed.

## Control-flow validation (2026-10-15)

The registered `aap-control-flow-v1` host test compiles the actual headless
AAP emulator and runs three deterministic short PDP-6 guest programs:

- A direct `JRST 1002` skips address 1001, executes `MOVEI 1,1` at 1002,
  then halts at 1003. The three observed IT0/ET0A milestone pairs have
  PCs 1000, 1002 and 1003.
- An indirect `JRST @1010` takes its destination from word 1010 containing
  1002 and follows the same PC sequence. This validates this indirect
  addressing case, not every indexed/indirect combination.
- `XCT 1010`, where 1010 holds `MOVEI 1,1`, emits **two ET0A events** for
  one IT0 at PC 1000: first opcode 256 (XCT), then 201 (MOVEI). The next
  IT0 is at PC 1001 and the terminating JRST follows.

The extractor now handles this observed nesting pattern: the outer
`XCT` owns the single `IT0`-to-next-`IT0` interval, while the additional
`ET0A` is recorded as `nested_opcodes_octal` on that outer record. Thus
the target's execution is accounted for **once**, not treated as a second
independent instruction interval. An incomplete `XCT` or an unexplained
additional `ET0A` is rejected. This models the *observed AAP milestone
semantics*, not a universal claim that all interrupt or exotic XCT flows
are covered. The final instruction remains unmeasured without a following
IT0. No per-opcode timing weights are generated by this trace alone.

## Conditional branch and loop guest verification (2026-10-15)

The registered `aap-branches-v2` suite builds the actual AAP emulator and
runs five short programs. `JUMPE` takes a branch, `JUMPN` falls through,
`SKIPE` skips a halt on a zero operand, `AOBJN` loops exactly once from a
left-half count of -2 before terminating at zero, and `AOBJP` branches after
a negative left half becomes zero. The suite validates the sequence of
executed PCs/opcodes, monotonic timing events, and every complete
IT0-to-next-IT0 interval. It observes 3, 3, 2, 3, and 2 intervals
respectively. The last halt is excluded, as before. These cases validate
control-flow decisions through observed PCs, not independent readback of
final accumulator/memory state. Opposite skip/AOBJ outcomes, wider loop
counts, and exceptional flows remain to be exercised.

## Independent branch guest-state verification (2026-10-15, v3)

`aap-branches-v3` extends the earlier branch tests with `SKIPE` not taken,
`AOBJP` not taken, and `AOBJN` starting with left-half -1 and -4.
The tested AOBJN loop executes respectively one, two, and four increments.
Each program stores its final accumulator or chosen branch result in
location 1020 and uses the emulator console's memory examination after
the guest halt. The suite checks that result against a separately specified
36-bit octal expectation as well as executed PCs, opcodes, and strict
complete logical-tick intervals. Nine guests yield 33 intervals overall;
terminating halts are unmeasured. Both halves of AOBJ accumulators increment
together, including the right half on an untaken branch. The earlier v2
suite remains registered as historical independent coverage. The full
JUMP/SKIP predicate space, AOBJ edge conditions, and indirect-address
variants remain open.

## Indexed and multilevel indirect guest accesses (2026-10-15)

The registered `aap-addressing-v1` suite runs five guest programs using
direct, indexed, single-level indirect, multilevel indirect, and indexed
indirect memory addressing. The fixtures distinguish pointer words from
data words using different contents, and store the loaded value to guest
location 1020. The console memory readback independently validates the
expected 36-bit result. Strict trace parsing checks instruction PCs,
opcodes, monotonic milestones, and complete IT0-to-next-IT0 intervals.
These five cases produce 2, 3, 2, 2, and 3 complete intervals. No timing
constants are inferred for opcode families or memory configurations.
Accumulator/fast-memory variation, alternative memory configurations,
and error/interrupt cases are still outstanding.

## Accumulator versus core and capacity variation (2026-10-15)

`aap-memory-paths-v1` executes the same four MOVE addressing workloads
with `cmem161C` 16K-word or `moby` 256K-word core, both with attached
`fmem162`. Eight cases independently verify a stored 36-bit guest result,
strict execution milestone ordering and complete IT0 intervals. For the
observed emulator revision/configuration, AC-reference MOVE and direct
core MOVE each measure 1701 logical ticks; indexed MOVE takes 2251,
and two-level indirect MOVE 2701. These values are identical between
the two core capacities tested. This demonstrates repeatable relative
model costs only; it does not measure physical PDP-6 timing or cover
fast-memory attachment changes and other memory organizations. The AC
fixture addresses accumulator 2 through the instruction effective address,
and does not independently time a changed or detached fast-memory module.

## Split 16K core mapping and absent fast memory (2026-10-15)

`aap-memory-map-v1` adds a third configuration containing two separately
attached `cmem161C` modules at core banks 0 and 1. An additional guest
reads word 040010 from bank 1 and stores 000000000654 in word 1020;
console readback, instruction trace, and strict tick intervals all pass.
All 13 enabled guest combinations pass. The mapped direct MOVE interval
was 1701 modeled ticks in this run, equal to the other direct accesses.
An experimental configuration omitting the `fmem162` attachment caused an
AAP emulator segmentation fault before successful guest validation. It is
explicitly excluded from the enabled suite, not counted as a pass; it
requires debugging in the PDP-6 emulator before timing use.

## No-fast-memory regression resolved (2026-10-15)

The segmentation fault noted above was traced to `pdp6/emu/mem.c`,
`wakemem()`: the fast-memory select path dereferenced an absent module.
PDP-6 emulator commit `44f3dc1` guards the optional device and routes
nonresponding fast-memory selections through the existing core-memory path.
The registered `aap-memory-no-fast-v1` regression executes four guest
addressing modes for each of the `cmem161C` 16K and `moby` 256K core
configurations with no fast-memory module. All eight passed independent
36-bit guest-result verification, opcode/PC tracing, and complete tick
interval extraction. In the observed model all four measured MOVE intervals
match the attached-fast-memory fixtures. Other memory errors and exceptional
executions remain unvalidated.

## Nested XCT and control-flow execution (2026-10-15)

`aap-xct-v2` executes three real guest fixtures: nested `XCT` of
`MOVEI`, `XCT` of a core-memory `MOVE`, and `XCT` of taken `JRST`.
Each has two complete intervals and an independently checked final
guest memory word. The extractor associates `256,201`, `200`, or `254`
with the outer `XCT` interval as appropriate; it does not fabricate
extra top-level execution intervals for nested target instructions.
The tick-extractor unit tests additionally reject unfinished nested
`XCT` and inconsistent nested instruction PCs. Interrupts, exceptions,
and aborted targets still require separate validation before using
these intervals for general timing comparisons.

## Exceptional trace validation batch 1 (2026-10-15)

The registered `aap-exception-traces-v1` regression supplies ten
deliberately malformed synthetic traces: absent events, orphan ET0A,
unpaired IT0, double IT0, execution-PC mismatch, equal logical ticks,
decreasing ticks, extra ET0A, unfinished nested XCT, and an unknown event.
All ten must fail with `ValueError`; passing means rejection, not timing
measurement. The extractor now requires strictly increasing ticks between
consecutive milestones. These tests do not establish behavior for real
interrupts, PI cycles, memory faults, I/O or aborted guest execution.

## Reproducible fifty-case guest batches (2026-10-15)

`aap-batch50-v1` builds AAP once and executes 50 isolated PDP-6 guest
programs: ten operand values each for direct, indexed, single-level
indirect, multilevel indirect, and MOVEI/ADDI arithmetic. The expected
36-bit guest result is computed from the fixture operation, independently
of the simulator output. Every case validates its memory readback,
IT0/ET0A sequence, executed PCs/opcodes, and complete positive tick
intervals. All 50 passed with the attached `fmem162` and `moby` core.
The five workload-specific total complete-interval costs were 3402, 5353,
3902, 4402, and 5053 AAP logical ticks respectively, constant across
these small input-value ranges. They are not universal opcode timings;
each total includes other guest instructions and excludes the final halt.
The next batches should prioritize distinct execution paths rather than
simply increasing the number of values per instruction.

## 100-case batch 5: BLT timing continuation semantics (2026-10-15)

## Trace instrumentation repeatability verification (2026-10-15)

`test-aap-instrumentation-v1.py` runs 20 real AAP guest configurations
with trace disabled and twice with it enabled: 60 emulator executions.
Each configuration produced identical 36-bit memory results, and both

## Deterministic guest-stop and transparency verification (2026-10-15)

The PDP-6 AAP console command `cpustatus` (emulator revision
`ac0e7a0`) responds from the simulator thread with `APR_STATUS_V1`
`START_PENDING`, `RUNNING` or `HALTED`. The reusable
`tools/aap_guest_console_v1.py` helper waits for HALTED before reading
guest memory, replacing host sleeps in all 23 registered Python timing
batches. Re-running all 1800 guest cases passed and produced identical
per-instruction complete-tick vectors to the earlier fixed-wait runs.

`test-aap-instrumentation-v1.py` independently executed 20 guest
workloads (60 runs: traced twice and untraced once per case), checking
equivalent guest results and identical per-interval tick vectors for
the traced repetitions. All passed under the HALT handshake. These
results establish practical reproducibility for the measured cases,
not asynchronous device/PI timing or full linked-DAIMOS behavior.
